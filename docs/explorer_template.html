Does rapid resonant imaging still work at 1 G?

A simulation of the erasure-imaging scheme of Holland et al. (arXiv:2406.02391). It models dark states in N=1 and heating in a deep tweezer, photon counting with 2–4% collection, and the off-resonant Raman scattering it causes on a qubit parked in N=0.

1 · Brightness

A 1 G field breaks the dark states the laser keeps making

With a fixed polarisation, 12 ground sublevels share 4 excited ones, so at least 8 combinations never absorb. Something has to rotate them back into the light. At B = 0 the only thing that does this is the tweezer's own tensor light shift. At 1 G, Larmor precession (0.4–1.1 MHz) adds a second, faster mixing. Move the angle χ between the imaging and tweezer polarisations to see where each works.

R₁ (10⁶ s⁻¹) at s_tot = 1, Δ = 0, in a 1.5 mK tweezer, with B ∥ tweezer polarisation. The dashed line is your χ. Hover to read a value.

Cut at the chosen χ. The narrow dips are two-photon resonances, where the Zeeman shift cancels a tensor-shift difference and a coherent dark state comes back.

3 · The cross-talk budget

Raman probability is fluence times a fixed rate

The imaging light sits 19.2 GHz below X(N=0)→A(J′=½,−) and 52 GHz below A(J′=3/2,−). A 1 G Zeeman shift of 1.4 MHz is 7×10⁻⁵ of that, so the N=0 cross section is the same at 0 and 1 G. With the Kramers–Heisenberg amplitudes summed coherently over both intermediate levels:

\[ P_{\rm Raman} = r_R\, s_{\rm tot}\, T, \qquad r_R \simeq \frac{\Gamma}{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2\Delta_0}\right)^{2}\kappa \approx \ {\rm s^{-1}}, \qquad \frac{P_{\rm Raman}}{N_{\rm photons}} = \frac{r_R}{R_1/s_{\rm tot}} \]

So cross talk per detected photon is set by one number, the imaging efficiency R₁/s_tot: photons per unit fluence. Dark states are what lower it, which is why the magnetic field matters for cross talk even though it does nothing to N=0 directly. Of the Raman events, about half go to N=2, 45% change F or m_F within N=0, and 3% go to v ≥ 1.
